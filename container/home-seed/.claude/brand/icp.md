## Primary persona: the founder paying pipeline tax

### Who they are

- **Roles in the door:** Founder, CEO, Co-founder. The person whose name is on the cap table and who is, by default, the head of growth because nobody else is.
- **Stage:** Series A B2B SaaS, $1M to $10M ARR. Post-PMF (they have customers and repeatable-ish sales) but pre-repeatable-revenue-machine. The growth engine is not built yet.
- **The tell:** they still log into Google Ads themselves. CPA dashboard looks reasonable. Pipeline doesn't match. They've never pulled the MQL-to-SQL rate by campaign. They ARE the marketing department.
- **Spend reality:** real monthly paid media, enough that a 20% CPA cut moves the P&L. Segmented under $25K / $25-75K / $75K+.

### The pipeline tax problem they're living

The founder's CPA is $100. Their MQL-to-SQL rate is 10%. Their actual cost per qualified lead is $1,000. They don't know this. The dashboard shows $100 and calls it a win.

Pipeline tax is the gap between what Google counts as a conversion and what their CRM proves became qualified pipeline. The algorithm keeps learning the wrong signal. Every dollar spent makes it worse. There are five specific structural leaks:

1. Google is optimizing to form fills, not SQLs
2. Closed-won deals never flow back to the algorithm with GCLIDs
3. 7-day attribution window on a 90-day sales cycle
4. No Customer Match or CRM ICP uploads — Smart Bidding optimizes to whoever converts, not whoever buys
5. No MQL-to-SQL rate by campaign — the 80-MQL / 0-SQL campaign looks fine

This is the thing GTMVP names and fixes. Not "wasted spend." Not "bad signal quality." **Pipeline tax.**

### What they care about (in priority order)

1. **Is the spend backing out?** Does paid media actually produce pipeline, and through which campaigns?
2. **A real CPQL number** — cost per qualified lead, not just CPA. The number their board is actually asking about.
3. **Getting a senior operator** without a $200K CMO hire or an agency retainer.
4. **Getting their own time back.** Running ads is not their zone of genius.
5. **A defensible answer** for the board on where pipeline comes from.
6. **Speed.** A fix this month, not a strategy deck next quarter.

### What they're tired of

- Agency QBRs that show CPA and never show CPQL or SQL rate by campaign
- Fractional CMOs who became slide-deck factories and left the pipeline tax running
- Being the bottleneck on their own paid media
- Dashboards nobody opens after week three
- Guessing, then defending the guess in board decks
- A good CPA and a bad pipeline month with no explanation for why

### What they already know (don't explain these)

- ROAS, CPA, CAC, LTV, attribution windows, MQL/SQL, tCPA/tROAS, Smart Bidding
- The difference between leading and lagging indicators
- That their CRM data is dirty and their agency isn't telling them everything
- That $100 CPA and "we're generating pipeline" don't always mean the same thing

### What they don't know yet (the gap GTMVP fills)

- That there's a name for why their CPA looks fine and their pipeline doesn't: pipeline tax
- That their actual CPQL could be 5x–10x their CPA
- Which of the five structural leaks is their biggest pipeline tax source
- How to get the GCLID loop closed so the algorithm learns from SQLs, not form fills

## Attribyte ICP

> Use this section any time you're writing for or about Attribyte (attribyte.xyz). This is a separate product with a different buyer, a different pain, and a different sales motion than GTMVP.
>
> **Critical positioning:** Attribyte is NOT "attribution software." It is how to end your pipeline tax and put more money back in your pocket. The GTMVP founder eventually grows into the Attribyte ICP, or refers peers to it.

### Entity sentence

> **"AttriByte shows which marketing touchpoints actually move pipeline and revenue — it's how to end your pipeline tax and put more money back in your pocket."**

### One-line ICP (Attribyte)

> **VP Marketing, CMOs, and RevOps leaders at mid-market to enterprise B2B SaaS ($10M to $200M+ ARR) who are making budget decisions across multiple paid channels and need to see which spend actually reached qualified pipeline — not just which spend got attributed credit.**

### Who they are

- **Roles:** VP Marketing, CMO, Head of Demand Gen, VP RevOps, Director of Marketing Analytics. The person who owns the budget line and has to justify it to the CFO or board.
- **Stage:** Mid-market to enterprise B2B SaaS. They have a real marketing stack (multiple ad platforms, CRM, maybe a CDP), real spend across channels, and a data team or RevOps function — but the measurement layer is broken or fragmented.
- **The pipeline tax problem at their level:** They're running multi-channel paid. They have a blended ROAS number. They have no clean CPQL number by channel and campaign. They can't tell the CFO which spend actually produced SQLs. The attribution tools give them credit assignment, not pipeline quality measurement.
- **Company size:** 50 to 500 employees is the sweet spot. Large enough to have multi-channel complexity; small enough that stitching together three separate tools is a genuine ops burden.

### What they care about (in priority order)

1. **CPQL by channel and campaign** — cost per qualified lead, not just CPA or attributed ROAS. The number that proves which spend reached pipeline.
2. **Incrementality that holds up in a CFO meeting** — not a correlation, a holdout-tested lift number.
3. **A single source of truth on attribution** — one number per channel, not three conflicting answers from three tools.
4. **Privacy-first measurement** — cookieless primary path, deterministic where possible.
5. **MMM at the portfolio level** — understanding spend efficiency across the whole mix.
6. **Consolidation** — replacing 2-3 point tools with one platform.

### What they're tired of

- Attribution tools that show credit assignment but not pipeline quality
- Running MTA in one tool, MMM in a spreadsheet, and incrementality tests manually — and getting three different answers
- Telling the CFO "it depends on how you count it"
- Third-party cookies breaking attribution every 6-12 months
- Tools that require a data engineer to maintain before any marketer can use them

### What they already know (don't explain these)

- Last-click attribution is broken; they've known for years
- MTA, MMM, and incrementality testing as distinct concepts
- ROAS, CPA, blended ROAS, CAC, LTV
- The difference between correlation and causation in marketing measurement
- That their CFO doesn't trust their attribution numbers

### What they don't know yet (the gap Attribyte fills)

- That all three methods (MTA + MMM + incrementality) can run in one platform with a shared data model
- That the real question isn't "which touchpoint gets credit" — it's "which touchpoints move pipeline, and what does that CPQL look like by channel"
- That deterministic-primary identity resolution can work without third-party cookies as the primary path
- That connecting attribution credit to CRM pipeline stages gives them CPQL and SQL rate by channel, not just cost per attributed lead

### The pain, verbatim

> **"We're running on three tools and getting three different answers. I can't walk into a CFO meeting and say 'it depends on the model.' I need one number I can defend — and that number needs to be cost per qualified lead, not cost per attributed click."**

### Buying triggers

- CFO or board is questioning marketing ROI and demanding a CPQL number, not just ROAS
- A major privacy change (iOS update, cookie deprecation wave) just broke their current measurement setup
- They're about to expand into a new channel and need to know if it's incremental before committing budget
- A new VP Marketing or CMO joined and immediately flagged the measurement mess as a top-3 priority
- They're consolidating vendors and marketing measurement is on the list

### Disqualifiers: who Attribyte is NOT for

- **Pre-revenue / single-channel.** If they're only running one ad platform with minimal spend, the complexity isn't there yet.
- **GTMVP-stage founders** (Series A, still logging in themselves). Wrong stage and wrong pain — refer them to GTMVP first.
- **Pure B2C / e-commerce.** Attribyte is built for B2B SaaS paid-media mechanics.
- **Companies with a mature, well-staffed data science team** running custom MMM in-house. They already solved it.
- **Anyone expecting a self-serve no-touch onboarding.** Attribyte requires connecting ad accounts and a light integration.

### Language they use (mirror this)

- *pipeline tax, CPQL, SQL rate, blended attribution, incrementality, holdout test, lift, media mix, first-party data, deterministic, probabilistic, cookieless, channel ROI, budget allocation, CFO asks, marketing mix*
- *"we're arguing about the numbers," "depends on the model," "last-click is lying to us," "we can't prove incrementality," "our CPQL is actually..."*
- *multi-touch, MTA, MMM, data clean room, identity resolution*
