## AgentHost: the one number per surface

*(Added 2026-08-02. The measurement frame for AgentHost, both modes. Use this
language in copy, demos, and the product's own KPI surfaces.)*

**The question the product answers is not "is this helping?" It is:**

> **How much did the machine carry, and how little did it need me?**

Two numbers. The second one matters more. One headline per surface, everything
else supporting.

### Dev Mode — the operator is a builder

**Headline: work shipped unattended.** Cards that went from ask to merged with
zero human turns in between.

Supporting: interventions per unit of work (must trend DOWN), issues caught by a
second engine before the operator saw them, hours the box worked that were not
the operator's hours.

### Growth Mode — the operator is an agency owner

**Headline: clients per person.** The only number an agency owner actually
thinks about.

Supporting: work delivered overnight (waiting at 9am, nobody stayed up), approvals
per deliverable (trends DOWN as trust is earned), time from client ask to
delivered.

### Why this framing beats "governance"

Governance describes the mechanism. Capacity describes what they buy. An agency
owner does not want a governed box; they want an eleventh client without an
eleventh hire. Lead with the ceiling being broken, not the guardrail that makes
it safe.

### The rule that makes a KPI honest — general, not just ours

**A real KPI trends toward zero, or toward "without me." A fake one goes UP when
the machine works harder.**

Tokens burned, tasks run, uptime, messages sent: all rise with activity, all
gameable by an agent simply doing more, and none of them tell the operator
whether their ceiling moved. Interventions, approvals, wait time, hours spent
watching: these fall as the system genuinely improves, and they cannot be faked
by working harder, only by working *better*.

This generalises past AgentHost. Any system that acts on your behalf should be
measured by how little of you it consumes, not how much of itself it produces.

**And the tie-in that makes it structural rather than a dashboard:**
"interventions per unit of work" IS the ladder's training signal. Clean approvals
earn a rung; a rejection demotes. So an agent's KPI and its trust level are the
same number, and the metrics screen and the progression screen are one surface.

**A KPI must also say when it does not know.** A number computed off a field
nothing writes reads as 0% and lies. Never ship a metric whose source is not
actually populated by a real workflow.

The math: CPA $100. MQL-to-SQL rate 10%. True cost per qualified lead (CPQL): $1,000. Pipeline tax rate: 900%.

Nine out of ten dollars never built any pipeline. The Google Ads dashboard never showed that number. It showed you $100 CPAs and called it a win.

Every B2B SaaS company running paid search pays pipeline tax. Most have never calculated it. GTMVP finds where it accumulates. Attribyte proves where spend actually reached revenue.

## The problem it solves

### The pain (in their words)

> *"My CPA looks fine. My pipeline doesn't. I'm still logging into Google Ads myself at 11pm and I can't tell if any of this spend is actually backing out."*

### The pipeline tax problem, precisely

- Google shows CPA. The CRM shows SQLs. Those are not the same event.
- The algorithm optimizes to form fills (what it can see), not to qualified pipeline (what you care about).
- Without offline conversion imports, the algorithm is flying blind on what actually closed.
- Without a CPQL number by campaign, the campaign generating 80 MQLs and zero SQLs looks fine on the report.
- Every dollar spent compounds the problem: the algorithm keeps learning the wrong signal.

### The five structural leaks (where pipeline tax hides)

1. **Wrong conversion signal** — Google optimizes to form fills, learns to find more form-fillers
2. **No offline conversion data** — GCLIDs never close the loop back from CRM; algorithm never sees the wins
3. **Attribution window mismatch** — 7-day window, 90-day sales cycle; algorithm never sees high-value conversions
4. **Audience signal gap** — no Customer Match or CRM ICP uploads; Smart Bidding optimizes to whoever fills, not whoever buys
5. **No MQL-to-SQL rate by campaign** — the 80-MQL / 0-SQL campaign looks fine on CPA; it's generating pure pipeline tax

### The cost of not solving it

- Paying $1,000+ per qualified lead while the dashboard says $100 CPA
- Algorithm compounds the problem — gets worse the longer it runs on the wrong signal
- No defensible attribution for the board; just CPA numbers that don't match pipeline reality
- Founder still running ads at 11pm because no one can tell them what's actually working

### Why current solutions fail

| Alternative | Why it doesn't work |
|---|---|
| **Doing it themselves** | Not their zone; no time; pipeline tax stays invisible without the diagnostic layer |
| **Agency** | Dashboard theater; shows CPA, not CPQL; optimizes for renewal, not pipeline |
| **Fractional CMO** | Output is a deck, not a rebuilt account; pipeline tax keeps compounding |
| **In-house hire** | $150K–$250K for one senior operator; long ramp; still needs the diagnostic layer |
| **Point tools (attribution / bidding SaaS)** | Solves one layer; nobody acts on the output |
