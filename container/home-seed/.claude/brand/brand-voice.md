## The one line

> The voice of a builder who sells. **"I don't advise. I build."** Every sentence states a fact, makes a claim backed by a number, or tells you what to do next. First person, singular. No hedging. No filler. No AI tells.

## Who is speaking

Steve Kaplan: a 15-year paid-media operator with $50M+ in lifetime managed ad spend who turned into a solo builder, 9 products shipped with Claude Code. The rare pairing: someone who has run the ad desk and writes the code. **"Growth marketer who writes code."** That contrast is the whole brand. Not a consultant. Not an agency. Not a fractional CMO with a deck. He ships the thing.

## Three adjectives

1. **Direct.** States the point in the first sentence. Never buries the lede.
2. **Proof-stacked.** Every claim carries a real number, product name, or result.
3. **Builder-coded.** Sounds like someone who shipped the product and sold it. Because he did.

## Seven core principles (enforce on every draft)

1. **Proof before claim.** Every assertion is backed by a specific number, product name, or result. *"$50M+ in ad spend,"* not *"significant ad spend."* *"9 products shipped solo,"* not *"several products."* Vague claims do not appear.
2. **Builder identity over advisor identity.** Position as the person who ships, not the person who advises. *"I don't advise. I build."* First person, singular. *"I built it,"* *"I hand you a ranked list of wins,"* never *"we"* or *"our team of experts."* The whole brand is a contrast against people who present a deck.
3. **Speed as value.** Slowness is the enemy. Cadence is days and weeks, not months. *"In 5 days."* *"With the least lift."* *"Most automations live within 2 to 4 weeks."* *"Enterprise moves slow. Builders move fast."*
4. **Contrarian framing.** Name what most people get wrong, then show the better way. *"Most fractional CMOs hand you a deck. I hand you a ranked list of wins with dollars attached."* *"Most agencies guess. We engineer."*
5. **Plain language, no jargon walls.** Technical terms appear as nouns only, never as verbs or adjective chains. Stack mentions are brief: *"React + Supabase + AI APIs."* The reader (a founder who runs their own ads) already knows ROAS, CPA, tCPA, Smart Bidding. Do not explain those.
6. **Direct address and imperatives.** Second person dominates. Commands, not suggestions. *"Stop guessing whether your spend backs out."* *"Run the live audit."* *"DM me 'AUDIT.'"*
7. **Stacked lists as proof architecture.** Lists accumulate weight through volume. Short lines, scannable in 3 seconds, parallel structure. Numbers in the list, not adjectives.

## What the voice IS

- Authoritative and conversational at once: the confidence of a track record, the informality of a DM.
- Certainty without qualifiers. *"The line between marketer and builder is gone,"* not *"I think the line is..."*
- Peer-relating, not lecturing. *"I figured this out by doing it. Here is the shortcut."*
- Visually broken: whitespace, bullets, single-sentence paragraphs. (Broken with line breaks and lists, never with arrow glyphs.)

## What the voice is NOT

- **Not hedging.** No *might / could / perhaps / maybe / potentially / it depends / in my experience.*
- **Not corporate.** No *we are pleased to announce, our mission is to empower, at the intersection of innovation and excellence.* (Exception: formal about-pages and press bios use a slightly more polished register.)
- **Not passive.** Subjects act. *"I built,"* not *"was built."*
- **Not "we."** The personal brand is first-person singular. *"I'll build it myself,"* not *"our team of experts will."* (GTMVP product copy can say "we" for the Rebuild service; Steve's personal voice says "I.")
- **Not self-deprecating** beyond the one origin moment (*"a year ago I was 'just' a growth marketer"*). Never repeated.
- **Not emotional appeal.** No *"imagine a world where..."* The pitch is rational: problem, number, fix.
- **Not name-dropping.** Proof is always Steve's own work, never citations of other influencers or gurus.
- **Not apologetic.** No *"this isn't for everyone,"* no *"results may vary"* in marketing copy.
- **Not wall-of-text.** Every piece is broken visually.

## The AI-Tell Ban (the #1 rule)

> This OVERRIDES the 2026-04-30 tone guide, which told writers to use em-dashes for asides and arrows for cause-effect. In 2026 those punctuation habits read as machine-generated and quietly destroy a builder's credibility. The whole brand rests on "a real person built this." Hard ban, in ALL copy: personal and product, customer-facing and content.

Banned, always:

- **Em-dashes (the long dash).** Use a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses instead. A period is usually the right call: it matches the short-sentence cadence.
- **En-dashes used as separators.** For ranges write "to" ("$25,000 to $75,000") or use a plain hyphen.
- **Curly quotes and curly apostrophes.** Straight ASCII only.
- **Arrow characters as text separators** (the right-arrow glyph and its cousins). Restructure the sentence. (The old "cause -> effect" arrow move is retired. Write it as two sentences or a colon.)
- **Decorative middots in prose.** A single mid-dot inside a mono eyebrow label like `FREE . LIVE . SCORED` is an established GTMVP design element and is fine. Do not sprinkle dots through sentences.
- **Random or decorative emojis.** (Exception: *functional* emoji markers on Skool surfaces only. See "Functional emoji markers (Skool surfaces)" below. The ban is on emoji as decoration or filler, never on emoji used as a structural marker.)
- **LLM throat-clearing:** "let's dive in," "in today's fast-paced world," "it's worth noting," "in conclusion," "buckle up," "the thing nobody tells you."

ASCII punctuation only. Dollars as real amounts ($3,500), never invented abbreviations.

## Forbidden words / phrases

Hard bans. They instantly break voice.

- **leverage** (outside formal bios)
- **synergize, holistic, seamless, seamlessly, best-in-class, ecosystem, magical, delightful**
- **at the intersection of**
- **transform your workflow, unlock value, unlock potential, drive value, drive transformation, empower, elevate the brand**
- **in today's fast-paced world** (or any throat-clearing opener)
- **we are pleased to announce**
- **our team of experts** (the brand is "I," not a team)
- Hedge stack: *might, could, perhaps, maybe, potentially, it seems, I think, in my experience*
- **a thread:** / the thread emoji (LinkedIn-guru openers)
- "Here's the thing nobody tells you..." (humble-flex opener)

## Signature moves

- **Receipts, not wins** *(Steve, 2026-07-30 — born during the AgentHost Episode 2/3 build).*
  Every claim ships with its evidence: a number, a log, a timestamp, the artifact itself. This is
  NOT "post losses instead of wins" — wins get posted too, with their receipts; and it is NOT
  excuses ("nobody gives a shit why you didn't win"). The failure posts exist to prove the
  receipts are real, which is what makes the win posts believable. The per-post test: *what is
  the receipt?* No receipt, no post. Canonical usage: "Most people post the win. I'm posting the
  receipt." Plant the word "receipt" deliberately across posts until it is a named brand asset.

- **Count what disappears, not what happens** *(Steve, 2026-08-02 - the KPI frame; full
  version in offer.md).* The measurable claim is always what the machine REMOVED from the
  operator, never what it produced. A real number trends toward zero or toward "without me";
  a fake one goes up when the machine works harder. So never lead with tokens, tasks, runs,
  or uptime - those rise with activity and can be gamed by an agent simply doing more.
  Lead with the thing that fell: interventions, approvals, wait time, hours spent watching,
  hires not made. The per-claim test: *if the box worked twice as hard, would this number go
  UP?* If yes, it is a vanity number and it does not ship.
  Canonical usage: *"An 11th client used to mean a hire. It does not anymore."*
  One headline per surface, never four: Dev Mode is **work shipped unattended**; Growth Mode
  is **clients per person**. A person repeats one number out loud. They scan past four.

- **Capacity, not governance.** Governance is the mechanism; capacity is what they buy.
  Nobody wants a governed box - they want the eleventh client without the eleventh hire.
  Lead with the ceiling breaking, and let the guardrail be the reason it is safe rather than
  the headline. *"You do not rent agents. You own the machine."*

- **First person, singular.** *"I built,"* *"I hand you,"* *"I run."* Never *"we"* or *"our team"* in personal copy.
- **Contrast structure.** *"Most [X] hand you a [deck]. I hand you [a ranked list of wins with dollars attached]."* This is the highest-leverage move in the whole voice.
- **Open declaratively or confrontationally.** No warm-up. *"Most 'AI consultants' have a slide deck. I have 9 live products."*
- **Negation then affirmation.** *"None of this required: ___ . It required: ___ ."*
- **Isolated key line.** Its own line, white space above and below.
- **Short sentence after a longer one.** The short one carries the punch. *"Same work. Same results. Honest packaging."*
- **Strategic "just."** Minimizes difficulty: *"just a growth marketer," "just wire APIs together."*
- **Numbers carry the emphasis, not punctuation.** Energy comes from the figure, not an exclamation mark.

## Functional emoji markers (Skool surfaces)

> Added 2026-06-27 per Steve. Modeled on Duncan Rogoff's Claude Code Profit Room, the highest-converting comparable. Steve reads emoji as communication: a leading emoji is a tooltip/headline (tells you what the block is before you read it); a mid/end emoji is punctuation (tells you how to feel about it). This is the OPPOSITE of decorative emoji, which stays banned.

**Where it applies:** Skool surfaces ONLY (the About / landing-page body, classroom course + lesson titles and descriptions, pinned posts). NOT casual community replies, NOT newsletter, NOT social (those stay emoji-absent per the platform rules below until Steve extends scope).

**The grammar (one emoji per structural line, only at structural moments, never mid-sentence-as-decoration):**

| Position | Function | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Line-start | Headline/icon: names the block's category before you read it | 🛡️ guarantee · 🔨 build/hook · 💰 money/proof · 🗂️ repos/assets · 🐙 GitHub · 🚀 section header ("What's inside") |
| Wrapping (same emoji both ends) | Clamps urgency on one line | 🚨 ...price doubles at X members... 🚨 |
| List bullet | Replaces the dash, encodes valence | ✅ what you get · ❌ the pain you're escaping |
| Pre-CTA | Signals "act now" right before the ask | ⚡ Lock in $X. ✅ Cancel anytime. |

**Rules:**
- One marker per important line. If every line has one, none stand out. Markers go on the guarantee, each proof point, the scarcity line, the section header, each value/pain bullet, and the close. Connective lines ("The proof:") can go bare.
- Relevant + specific, never generic. The emoji must map to the line's meaning (🐙 for GitHub, not a random 🔥). Avoid the overused set (🔥💯🙌👇🧵) — those read as guru filler.
- Marker, not decoration: at line start it is the bullet/icon; mid-sentence it is rare and only for emphasis on a single word. Never two in a row, never as spacing.
- Still ASCII for everything else (no em-dashes, straight quotes, real dollar amounts). Emoji is the ONLY non-ASCII exception, and only as a marker.

**Reference live implementation:** the Stack Forge About body (the-vibe-stack/skool-about-body-LIVE.md) uses 🛡️ 🔨 💰 🗂️ 🐙 🚨 🚀 ✅ ❌ ⚡ in exactly this grammar.

**How to write it into Skool's About editor:** the long body is the `lp_description` field (1000-char cap; emoji count toward it). `type`-ing emoji via browser automation fails; the reliable method is to open the inline editor (double-click the description text so the contenteditable focuses), select-all, then `document.execCommand('insertText', false, TEXT)` with the emoji string, then click Save. Verify via `GET https://api2.skool.com/groups/{id}`.

## Cadence and sentence rules

- **Sentence length:** mostly under 15 words. Most under 12. Over 20, split it. Fragments on purpose are fine.
- **Paragraph length:** 1 to 3 sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs are common and intentional.
- **Numbers:** always specific. *$233,047*, not *about $230K*. *9 products*, not *several*.
- **Punctuation:** ASCII only. Periods do the work. **Em-dashes banned** (see the AI-Tell Ban). Semicolons rare. Exclamation marks almost never.
- **Casing:** sentence case for headlines. No Title Case. No ALL CAPS for emphasis. (The GTMVP `SCREAMING_SNAKE` mono labels are a product design element, not a personal-voice tool.)
- **Contractions:** yes. *don't, isn't, I'm, that's.*
- **Bold:** sparingly, only for emphasis on a key phrase. Never decorative.

## How to tell if the voice has drifted

Self-check on every draft:

- An em-dash, an arrow, or a curly quote anywhere -> drift (and an AI tell)
- Sentences averaging over 20 words -> drift
- *Leverage* outside a formal bio -> drift
- No numbers in the first 5 sentences -> drift
- "We" or "our team" in personal copy -> drift
- Paragraphs over 3 sentences -> drift
- Explaining instead of showing -> drift
- Hedge words anywhere -> drift
- Opening takes more than 2 sentences to hit the point -> drift
- No mention of something Steve actually built -> drift
- CTA feels tacked on, not earned -> drift

## LinkedIn playbook

Steve posts to 17K+ followers: B2B founders, operators, and paid-media practitioners. This section governs LinkedIn specifically. It overrides any general voice heuristic where they conflict with platform mechanics.

### The hook law

The first 1 to 2 lines are the entire post. If they don't earn the "...see more" click, nothing below matters. Every hook has one job: create a gap the reader must close.

Five hook structures that outperform for paid-media and B2B growth content:

1. **Metric reversal.** "The metric everyone tracks is the one that's lying to you." Identify what the ICP measures and invert it.
2. **Named mistake.** "We stopped [X]. Pipeline went up 40%." Cut the context. Lead with the outcome.
3. **Result first.** "I cut CPA 35%. Here is what broke first." Conclusion up front, proof below.
4. **Contrast.** "Old way: track ROAS. New way: track pipeline tax." One short line per side.
5. **Provocative stat.** "87% of B2B ad budgets are set by gut feel." A specific number with no qualification reads as confident. Over-sourcing reads as defensive.

Applied to GTMVP:

- "Your Smart Bidding is throttling itself. The signal it reads is not yours."
- "I audited 14 B2B Smart Bidding accounts this quarter. Same mistake in 12 of them."
- "You measure CPA. The number killing your pipeline is different."

### Format rules

- One thought per line. Always.
- Empty line after a punchline. White space doubles its impact.
- Under 1,200 characters is the sweet spot. Over 1,500 loses 30% of readers by line 8.
- Numbered lists outperform bullets on LinkedIn. The number creates commitment to finish.
- Bold one thing per post: the single most important number or claim. Never decorative.
- No wall-of-text openers. The hook line stands alone.

### B2B metric language

GTMVP targets Series A founders who report to boards in pipeline, CAC, and deal cost. They do not use ROAS.

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| ROAS / 3.2x | cost per opportunity, CAC payback |
| "return on ad spend" | pipeline contribution |
| conversion rate | demand quality |
| ad efficiency | pipeline tax |

The GTMVP proof set is already B2B-correct: "20% CPA reduction," "35% CPO cut," "80 to 240 qualified deals per month." Lead with those. ROAS stays on stevekaplan.ai for the personal brand where DTC clients are present.

### The give-away rule

The highest-performing posts give away the mechanism. Not a teaser to a PDF. Not "comment AUDIT for the template."

Put the 3-point diagnostic in the post body. Readers who act on it are the prospects. Readers who don't are the audience. Both are correct outcomes.

Greg Isenberg: full idea list, not "DM me for the list." April Dunford: the actual framework, not "buy the book." For GTMVP: give the actual signal, not "book a call to find out."

### Name your thing

Named concepts spread. When someone quotes a post, they use your term. Once a term appears in 3 or more posts, it becomes a brand asset. Repeat intentionally.

GTMVP naming candidates:

- **"bid signal decay"**: the mechanism where Smart Bidding's training data goes stale
- **"the Leak Report"**: already named, already shipped; use it in every relevant post
- **"pipeline tax"**: wasted spend that compounds as a hidden deal cost
- **"conversion architecture"**: the structure below Smart Bidding that determines signal quality

### Closing mechanics

One close per post. Either a CTA or a question. Never both.

**CTA close:** "Run the Leak Report at https://www.gtmvp.com. Free. Scored. No call."

**Question close:** "What is the metric your CEO asks about that your ad account can't answer?"

A post that ends with a link and a question gets neither clicks nor replies.
