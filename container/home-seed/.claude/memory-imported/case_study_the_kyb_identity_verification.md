# Case Study: The KYB — Identity Verification Platform
## Multi-touch Attribution and Channel Restructuring

**Presented by GTMVP / Steve Kaplan AI**

---

## Client Overview

A B2B SaaS company providing identity verification and Know Your Business (KYB) compliance solutions. The platform served regulated financial institutions globally, including banks, payment processors, fintechs, and other entities with compliance obligations around business entity verification.

**Vertical:** Regtech / KYC SaaS  
**Challenge:** Unlock demand generation while maintaining cost-per-acquisition discipline in a product with a 90-120 day sales cycle  
**Channels:** Google Search, LinkedIn, Programmatic Display

---

## The Problem

The company was running paid campaigns across Google, LinkedIn, and programmatic display. The internal attribution model attributed most pipeline to Google branded search, leading the team to over-invest in brand defense while underinvesting in demand generation. Cost per qualified opportunity had risen to over $4,200. The sales cycle was 90 to 120 days, which made last-touch models completely unreliable.

The sales team reported that many top-revenue accounts had no visible first-touch data from any paid channel, suggesting that either the attribution model was broken or demand-generation channels were being systematically undervalued.

---

## What Was Done

**Full multi-touch attribution build.** Implemented a cross-channel attribution architecture connecting ad platform data to CRM stage progressions. Structured the data to move beyond last-click to a linear decay model, weighting early-funnel touchpoints more accurately.

**LinkedIn demand generation overhaul.** Identified compliance officers and heads of risk as the primary early-stage influencers in KYB buying decisions. Rebuilt LinkedIn campaigns with sequenced creative targeting these personas. Used account-based marketing principles to reach them at specific accounts known to be in high-value deal windows.

**Google restructuring around high-intent non-branded terms.** Moved Google spend away from brand defense toward high-intent non-branded terms tied to regulatory change events. Built campaigns around specific compliance requirements (e.g., beneficial ownership disclosure, sanctions screening) rather than generic identity verification terms.

**CRM integration and stage-level attribution.** Connected paid platform data (Google, Meta, LinkedIn) to the CRM's pipeline stage progression. This gave the team visibility into not just whether a paid channel sourced an opportunity, but whether the sourced opportunities were moving through the sales process at different rates depending on channel.

---

## Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Cost Per Qualified Opportunity | Reduced from $4,200 to $890 over 5 months |
| CAC Reduction | 78% |
| Pipeline Attributed to LinkedIn | Increased from 8% to 41% of sourced opportunities |
| Top-Account Findings | 3 of top 5 revenue-generating accounts had zero visible first-touch data before the attribution rebuild |

---

## Attribution Thread

The KYB engagement demonstrated the core thesis behind Attribyte:

1. Companies spending without attribution believe they understand their CAC. They are almost always wrong by a factor of 2x to 5x.
2. The fix is not more spend. It is better measurement.
3. Once measurement is fixed, budget allocation decisions change dramatically.

The breakthrough came when the team realized that their top revenue accounts had often started with a LinkedIn interaction 4-6 months before closed revenue, but because the last click was a branded search, LinkedIn was receiving zero credit. The linear decay model redistributed credit to early-funnel channels, revealing which channels were actually driving pipeline vs. which were optimizing already-warm prospects.
