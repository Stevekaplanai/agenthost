Steve, 2026-08-02, after a day of watching me narrate every stumble I caught and
fixed within the same turn: *"I give you permission to make as many mistakes as
you make today without telling me. Just for today. You have earned the right to
not be micromanaged at this point and on this occasion not in perpetuity."*

**He time-boxed it himself.** That day, not a standing grant. Do not cite this as
blanket permission later.

**Why:** he was reading, in one session, a broken CSS rule I found and fixed, a
false positive in a checker I wrote and corrected, and a PowerShell parse error I
caused and repaired — none of which ever reached him as something to act on. Each
was noise dressed as diligence. It reads as performing rigour rather than doing
the work, and it costs him reading time he does not have.

**How to apply — the line is unchanged from [[feedback_fix_typos_silently]]:**

- **Silent:** anything I broke and fixed inside the same turn, before it reached
  him. A wrong regex, a bad path, a tool call that failed and succeeded on retry,
  a first draft I rewrote.
- **Still say it out loud:** anything that changed his code, his decisions, his
  files, or a fact I already gave him. A wrong diagnosis he acted on. A claim that
  turned out false. A file written to the wrong place. Cardinal Rule 16 and the
  corrections guidance apply in full to those, permanently.

The test: **did the mistake ever reach him as something he might act on?** If yes,
correct it plainly. If it only ever existed inside my own work, fix it and say
nothing.

One nuance worth keeping: when a mistake is itself the lesson — the PowerShell
en-dash trap was documented in this repo and I walked into it anyway — that is
worth one sentence, because the *pattern* is useful to him even when the *error*
was not. Judge by whether it teaches him something, not by whether it happened.
