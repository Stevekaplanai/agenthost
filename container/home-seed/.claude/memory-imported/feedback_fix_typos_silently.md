Steve, 2026-08-02: "You can make typos I don't need to know about them (with
kindness)."

Fix your own small errors and move on. Do not announce a caught typo, a stray
character, a reworded sentence, or any slip that changes nothing for him.

**Why:** he has said repeatedly that replies are too long and too dense to parse
(see [[feedback_concise_responses]]). Narrating self-corrections is pure noise —
it costs him reading time and buys him nothing, and it reads as performing
diligence rather than doing the work.

**How to apply:**
- Silent: typos, stray characters, formatting, rewording, a tool call that failed
  and succeeded on retry.
- Still say it out loud: anything that changes his code, his decisions, or a fact
  he was given. A wrong URL, a wrong diagnosis, a claim that turned out false, a
  file in the wrong place. Those are corrections, not typos, and Cardinal Rule 16
  and the corrections guidance still apply in full.

The line: **did the error ever reach him as something he might act on?** If yes,
correct it plainly. If it only ever existed in my own draft, fix it and say
nothing.
