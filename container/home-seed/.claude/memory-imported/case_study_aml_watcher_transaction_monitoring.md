# Case Study: AML Watcher — Transaction Monitoring SaaS
## Paid Media Attribution Rebuild and CAC Reduction

**Presented by GTMVP / Steve Kaplan AI**

---

## Client Overview

A B2B SaaS company providing anti-money laundering (AML) screening, monitoring, and compliance workflow infrastructure. The platform served banks, fintechs, payment processors, and other regulated financial entities that are legally required to screen transactions and entities against sanction lists, PEP databases, and adverse media sources.

**Vertical:** Regtech / AML Compliance SaaS  
**Challenge:** Build a paid acquisition engine for a complex, compliance-driven product in a vertical where most buyers are not aware a better solution exists until they have a problem with their current one  
**Channels:** Google Search, LinkedIn  

---

## The Problem

AML compliance is not discretionary. The buyers have a legal obligation to solve the problem, but most of them have already solved it with whatever tool they found first or inherited from a predecessor. The incumbent inertia is significant.

Paid media had not been a meaningful acquisition channel. The company was generating most new business through word of mouth, industry events, and outbound sales. The brief was to build a paid program that could generate qualified pipeline without inflating the cost of acquisition to a level that made it economically unattractive relative to the existing channels.

The additional constraint was that the ICP was narrow. Not every bank or fintech is the right fit. Company size, regulatory jurisdiction, and transaction volume all affect whether the platform is a match. Running broad campaigns to a wide audience would generate leads, but most of them would not qualify.

---

## What Was Done

**ICP-first campaign architecture.** Started by working with the sales team to define the qualified buyer precisely: company type, size range, regulatory context, and the specific trigger events that typically caused a buyer to start evaluating a new AML solution. Built the campaigns around those specifications rather than the broadest possible keyword and audience set.

**Search campaign build around trigger events.** Identified the searches that indicate a buyer is at or near the trigger moment: regulatory examination preparation, vendor replacement due to false positive rates, product launches requiring new transaction monitoring. Built tightly themed campaign groups around these intent signals.

**LinkedIn ABM targeting.** Built account-based marketing campaigns on LinkedIn targeting specific company profiles and job titles within those companies. Compliance officers, heads of financial crime, and Chief Risk Officers at fintechs, challenger banks, and payment processors in the target size range.

**Sequential nurture structure.** For buyers who engaged with content but did not convert immediately, built a sequential ad program that served progressively more specific messaging as the buyer moved through the awareness and evaluation process. First exposure to the category problem, second exposure to the platform capabilities, third exposure to a specific offer.

**Conversion quality definition.** Defined what a qualified conversion looked like in detail with the sales team and rebuilt the paid media optimization targets to reflect actual qualified pipeline rather than raw form fills. Reduced the volume of leads while substantially improving the rate at which leads converted to qualified opportunities.

**Attribution from first paid touch to closed-won.** Worked with the marketing and sales teams to build the reporting infrastructure connecting the paid media activity to closed revenue. For a product with a multi-month sales cycle, last-touch attribution was systematically misleading. The rebuild gave the team a view of which campaigns and channels were contributing at each stage.

---

## Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Cost Per Qualified Opportunity | -61% over 6 months |
| Lead-to-SQL Conversion Rate | Improved from 9% to 31% |
| Paid as a Pipeline Source | Grew from under 5% to 28% of new pipeline |

---

## Attribution Thread

The AML Watcher engagement followed the same diagnostic process now formalized in GTMVP's GTM audit and Attribyte's attribution platform:

1. Connect cost data to revenue outcomes, not just leads or calls
2. Separate high-quality pipeline from volume metrics
3. Identify which spend is generating revenue and which is generating activity
4. Reallocate toward what works, cut what does not
5. Build a measurement layer that holds over time as channels and tactics change

The critical finding: the company was measuring CPL (cost per lead) and comparing it against the cost of their other channels, but most leads were not qualified. Once pipeline quality was separated from lead volume, the economics of paid media became clear.
